Selected watch-industry reporting, retained by date.

October 6, 2026
  1. 01

    Roger Dubuis Reimagines the Knights of the Round Table

    Roger Dubuis presents the thirteenth Excalibur Knights of the Round Table, combining twelve sculpted figures with the automatic RD821 calibre in one of the manufacture’s most theatrical mechanical compositions.

    Fratello

  2. 02

    Patek Philippe Turns Milan Into a Celebration of Mechanical Watchmaking

    Patek Philippe’s largest Watch Art Grand Exhibition brings together historic calibres, rare crafts and new complications, led by a platinum perpetual-calendar chronograph whose calendar indications jump simultaneously in 30 milliseconds.

    Wallpaper*

  3. 03

    Czapek Translates Boris Vian’s Surrealism Into Mechanical Form

    The 18-piece Promenade L’Écume des Jours combines enamel, mother-of-pearl, jade and miniature painting with Czapek’s micro-rotor Calibre SXH5.1, turning literary inspiration into a richly constructed mechanical watch.

    WatchTime

October 5, 2026
  1. 01

    Omega Gives James Bond’s Seamaster a Hero-and-Villain Pairing

    Omega introduces two Seamaster Diver 300M editions aligned with James Bond and Spectre, adding distinct 007 cues to the collection’s established mechanical-diver architecture. The pairing extends the cinematic narrative from its familiar hero to the opposing side without abandoning the Seamaster’s professional identity.

    Time+Tide

  2. 02

    Girard-Perregaux Takes the Laureato Onto the Formula 1 Grid

    Girard-Perregaux and Williams F1 introduce two 42mm Laureato models with Williams-blue Clous de Paris dials, powered by the automatic GP1800 and GP3300 chronograph calibres. The racing references remain contained within the Laureato’s established architecture rather than overwhelming its mechanical identity.

    WatchTime

  3. 03

    King Seiko Makes the Modern VANAC Lighter and More Assertive

    The new King Seiko VANAC uses a titanium case and bracelet to reduce weight while a fresh dial develops the bold geometry of the 1970s original. Rather than directly reproducing a vintage reference, it continues the more experimental side of King Seiko’s mechanical-watch identity.

    Revolution

October 3, 2026
  1. 01

    Hamilton Revives the Cushion Watch With Proper Hand-Wound Mechanics

    Hamilton revives its 1920s cushion watches in compact 30mm and 34mm cases powered by the hand-wound H-42 calibre. Preserving the small proportions and mechanical winding gives the collection more authenticity than a typical vintage-styled revival.

    Hodinkee

  2. 02

    Longines Sends the Spirit Pilot Collection Into the Blue

    Longines gives the 39mm Spirit Pilot and Spirit Pilot Flyback matte-blue dials. The Flyback retains a hand-wound, column-wheel flyback chronograph movement, preserving the collection’s legitimate aviation-timing heritage beneath the contemporary colour.

    Fratello

  3. 03

    Cartier Brings the Roadster Back Into the Sports-Watch Conversation

    After roughly 14 years away, the Cartier Roadster returns in Medium and Large sizes with its tonneau case, automotive dial and signature date magnifier intact. Automatic mechanical movements and 100 metres of water resistance restore it as one of Cartier’s most distinctive sporting designs.

    Time+Tide

October 2, 2026
  1. 01

    Patek Philippe Opens a New Chapter for the Perpetual Calendar Chronograph

    The new Ref. 5978P is only the seventh generation of Patek Philippe’s manually wound perpetual calendar chronograph and the first to combine instantaneous calendar and chronograph-minute mechanisms. Its debut edition uses a 42mm platinum case and is limited to 120 pieces.

    Hodinkee

  2. 02

    Christopher Ward Revisits Its First In-House Complication

    The C1 Jump Hour XV marks 15 years since the brand’s first in-house complication with a 39mm case, concentric guilloché dial and JJ01 automatic jump-hour calibre. Its 36-part module stores energy progressively to reduce shock to the escapement at the hour change.

    Time+Tide

  3. 03

    Bangkok Watch Week Strengthens Southeast Asia’s Watchmaking Platform

    The second Bangkok Watch Week gathered more than 41 brands and expanded public access through brand forums, vintage watches and mechanical complications. Exhibits from maisons including Bvlgari, Cartier and Blancpain showed the growing regional influence of the event.

    Revolution

October 1, 2026
  1. 01

    Ulysse Nardin Marks 25 Years of the Freak With Radical Mechanics and Traditional Craft

    Ulysse Nardin marks 25 years of the Freak with two 99-piece Freak One editions pairing its rotating movement with enamel and guilloché decoration. Both use the automatic UN-240 manufacture calibre, continuing the Freak’s challenge to conventional dials and hands.

    WatchTime

  2. 02

    Tudor’s North Flag Legacy Returns to the Center of Its Modern Identity

    Fratello revisits how the North Flag became Tudor’s first watch with an in-house movement in 2015 and considers why the new Northflag again matters strategically. Its distinctive design and COSC and METAS credentials reinforce Tudor’s independent technical identity.

    Fratello

  3. 03

    Toronto’s Expanding Watch Fair Shows the Strength of Collector-Led Events

    The third Toronto Timepiece Show gathered roughly 90 brands, including Grönefeld, Christopher Ward, Baltic and Hanhart. Its growth shows how public regional fairs are becoming important meeting points for mechanical-watch brands, independent makers and collectors.

    Hodinkee

  1. 01

    Gallet’s Return Tests the Swiss Industry’s Mid-Tier Ambitions

    Backed by Breitling’s House of Brands, Gallet returns with mechanical watches priced from roughly $2,900 to $6,700, including hand-wound B09-powered models. The relaunch is a broader test of whether established Swiss groups can reclaim territory surrendered to value-focused independents and microbrands.

    Hodinkee

  2. 02

    NOMOS Takes Its Worldtimer Into the Dark

    The Club Sport Neomatik Worldtimer Dark pairs a black dial and expanded luminous indications with the manufacture’s automatic world-time architecture. It joins the permanent collection as a more nocturnal interpretation of NOMOS’s practical travel watch.

    NOMOS Glashütte

  3. 03

    Panerai Connects a Florentine Centenary to Eight-Day Mechanics

    Panerai marks 100 years of its historic Florence boutique with an immersive exhibition and two 44mm Luminor Firenze editions. Both use the manually wound P.5000 calibre with an eight-day reserve, translating the maison’s Florentine and naval history into contemporary mechanical watches.

    Revolution

September 29, 2026
  1. 01

    Louis Vuitton Returns to Its First Watch in Ceramic

    The Monterey Ceramic revives the pebble-shaped, lugless design of Louis Vuitton’s 1988 watch in 37mm black or green ceramic cases. Its new automatic LFTMA01.02 calibre brings the unconventional form into the permanent collection as a fully mechanical proposition.

    Revolution

  2. 02

    Breguet Reframes the Marine Through Guilloché and Movement Detail

    The refreshed Marine 5517 and flyback Chronographe 5527 receive solid-gold, hand-guilloché dials, revised displays and subtle movement updates. Available in gold or titanium, the redesign strengthens the collection’s link to Breguet’s historic marine chronometers.

    Fratello

  3. 03

    Hamilton Takes Its Hand-Wound Field Watch Back Into the Game

    The 38mm Khaki Field Mechanical Modern Warfare 4 edition pairs a compact black-PVD case with Hamilton’s manually wound H-50 calibre and an 80-hour power reserve. Its restrained gaming references preserve the practical character of the underlying mechanical field watch.

    WatchTime

September 28, 2026
  1. 01

    Ulysse Nardin Brings Traditional Craft to the Freak’s Radical Mechanics

    The new Freak One editions combine the crownless, handless UN-240 carousel movement with blue enamel or hand-guilloché decoration. Limited to 99 pieces each, they place traditional métiers d’art over one of modern mechanical watchmaking’s most unconventional architectures.

    Fratello

  2. 02

    Jaeger-LeCoultre Makes the Power Reserve Part of the Architecture

    The Master Control Chronometre Date Power Reserve combines a 39mm integrated-bracelet case with the in-house automatic Calibre 738, COSC certification and a 70-hour reserve. Its balanced dial turns a practical indication into a defining visual element.

    Time+Tide

  3. 03

    Chronoswiss Gives Mechanical Digital Time a Softer Palette

    The Neo Digiteur Pastel Code presents jumping hours, minutes and seconds through three apertures, powered by the hand-wound C.85757 with a proprietary display module. Mint, Sky and Rose editions soften its architectural case without disguising the unconventional mechanics.

    WatchTime

    Vintage Character Returns to the Market

  1. 01

    Patek Philippe Takes the Celestial Further

    Patek Philippe’s Celestial Ref. 6105G combines a rotating Geneva sky chart with moon phase, sidereal time, date, and the manufacture’s first wristwatch display for sunrise and sunset. Beneath its unconventional 47mm white-gold form is a technically ambitious evolution of Patek’s astronomical-watch lineage.

    Hodinkee

  2. 02

    Formex Recasts the Pilot’s Watch in Titanium

    The AirField Automatic pairs a 40mm hardened Grade 2 titanium case with 150 metres of water resistance and a 65-hour Sellita-based mechanical movement. Its restrained aviation design focuses on legibility, durability, and practical everyday proportions.

    IWC Makes Its Perpetual Calendar Easier to Set

    The Big Pilot’s Watch Perpetual Calendar ProSet “Le Petit Prince” develops Kurt Klaus’s mechanically programmed calendar with IWC’s newer ProSet adjustment system. It preserves the complication’s synchronized displays while making correction and long-term ownership more approachable.

  1. 01

    Seiko adds olive-green and khaki-beige dials to the 39.4mm Seiko 5 Sports Field GMT, retaining the automatic 4R34 caller-GMT movement and fixed 24-hour bezel. The update keeps mechanical dual-time functionality approachable while giving the utilitarian design more character.

    Time+Tide

    Five Discontinued Watches That Still Deserve a Place in Today’s Market

    Vacheron Constantin celebrates the Overseas anniversary with a 200-piece white-gold perpetual calendar featuring an azure dial and the 4.05mm-thick automatic Calibre 1120 QP/1. At only 8.1mm thick overall, it keeps a major complication within the collection’s unusually slim sports-watch profile.

    Hodinkee
