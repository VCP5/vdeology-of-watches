Three stories,
kept in time.

Selected watch-industry reporting, retained by date.

September 25, 2026
  1. 01

    Seiko Gives Its Accessible Field GMT Two Earthier Identities

    Seiko adds olive-green and khaki-beige dials to the 39.4mm Seiko 5 Sports Field GMT, retaining the automatic 4R34 caller-GMT movement and fixed 24-hour bezel. The update keeps mechanical dual-time functionality approachable while giving the utilitarian design more character.

    Time+Tide

  2. 02

    Five Discontinued Watches That Still Deserve a Place in Today’s Market

    Fratello revisits five mechanical watches whose design, history or technical identity could justify a return to permanent production. The selection highlights how discontinued references can retain relevance—and sometimes expose gaps in a brand’s current catalogue.

    Fratello

  3. 03

    Vacheron Marks 30 Years of the Overseas with an Ultra-Thin Perpetual Calendar

    Vacheron Constantin celebrates the Overseas anniversary with a 200-piece white-gold perpetual calendar featuring an azure dial and the 4.05mm-thick automatic Calibre 1120 QP/1. At only 8.1mm thick overall, it keeps a major complication within the collection’s unusually slim sports-watch profile.

    Hodinkee

September 24, 2026
  1. 01

    Breguet Reframes the Marine with a More Classical Identity

    Breguet updates the automatic Marine 5517 and flyback-chronograph 5527 with Breguet numerals, finer “Quai de l’Horloge” guilloché dials and revised movements. The changes move its sportiest collection closer to the manufacture’s historical visual language without replacing the established cases.

    Hodinkee

  2. 02

    Citizen Pairs Damascus Steel with a Mechanical Traveler GMT

    The limited Series 8 NB6092-01E combines a Damascus-steel case, forged-carbon dial and automatic Calibre 9054 with an independently adjustable local-hour hand. Limited to 1,300 pieces, it gives Citizen’s modern mechanical line a stronger material identity.

    Citizen

  3. 03

    Longines Takes Its Spirit Pilot Collection into the Blue

    Longines expands the Spirit line with blue-dial versions of its 39mm automatic Pilot and hand-wound Pilot Flyback. Both use COSC-certified mechanical calibres, while the flyback adds a column-wheel chronograph and 68-hour power reserve without sacrificing compact proportions.

    WatchTime

September 23, 2026
  1. 01

    Héron Builds a Compact Explorer for Everyday Use

    Canadian independent Héron introduces the Continental, reworking explorer-watch language through a 37mm hardened-steel case and luminous sandwich dial. A Miyota 9039 automatic movement and beads-of-rice bracelet turn its vintage references into an approachable daily mechanical watch.

    Fratello

  2. 02

    A. Lange & Söhne Brings Two New Watches to the Concours Lawn

    A. Lange & Söhne presented new versions of the 1815 UP/DOWN and platinum Triple Split at the Concours of Elegance at Hampton Court. By placing complex mechanical watches within classic-car culture, the brand reinforces a shared language of engineering, craft and longevity.

    WorldTempus

  3. 03

    FounderFest Takes Independent Releases from Event to Global Window

    FounderFest’s seven independent-watch collaborations entered a 24-hour global ordering window before returning to in-store exclusivity in London, New York and Melbourne. The format connects a physical collector event with limited worldwide access, offering smaller makers an alternative release model.

    Time+Tide

September 22, 2026
  1. 01

    Baume & Mercier Gives the Clifton a Cooler Mechanical Register

    Baume & Mercier gives the 39mm Clifton Automatic Date an ice-blue guilloché dial while retaining the poised proportions of a 1950s dress watch. The Baumatic manufacture movement brings a five-day power reserve, making the new tone more than a surface change.

    WorldTempus

  2. 02

    Panerai Makes a Proper 42mm Luminor

    Panerai brings full tool-watch capability to a 42mm Luminor for the first time, retaining 500m water resistance and its signature crown-protection bridge. The three references use the automatic P.980 with a three-day reserve, proving smaller dimensions need not compromise capability.

    Fratello

  3. 03

    Venezianico Turns Lightness into an Italian Mechanical Statement

    At 48 grams, the Venezianico Redentore Levius reframes a dress watch through a titanium case and hand-turned guilloché dial. Its manual-wind V5000 is designed and made in Italy, turning the release into a concrete step toward a stronger domestic mechanical-watchmaking base.

    Time+Tide

September 21, 2026
  1. 01

    Bianchet Brings a New Ceramic Contrast to the UltraFino Skeleton

    Bianchet’s Tonneau UltraFino Ceramic Skeleton pairs deep-blue and white ceramic with an exposed ultra-thin mechanical architecture. The contrast reinforces the growing space for lightweight, sporty haute horlogerie.

    Fratello

  2. 02

    Jaeger-LeCoultre Finds Clarity in the Master Control Chronometre

    Jaeger-LeCoultre refines its classic date watch through a 38mm case, 8.4mm profile and integrated bracelet. Inside, the automatic Calibre 899 provides a 70-hour reserve and the technical foundation for a more restrained daily proposition.

    Time+Tide

  3. 03

    Dublin’s Chronography Gives Independent Watchmaking a New Stage

    Chronography brings independent and microbrand watchmaking into a focused one-day event for Irish collectors. Its format shows how smaller physical fairs can become meaningful discovery and sales channels beyond the traditional European circuit.

    WatchPro

September 20, 2026
  1. 01

    Christopher Ward Puts Mechanical Chronographs Back in the Sealander

    Christopher Ward returns to the mechanical chronograph with the C63 Sealander, ending a gap in non-quartz chronographs since 2020. Its Sellita SW510-1 automatic movement and 56-hour reserve bring complication back to the brand’s core price territory.

    Fratello

  2. 02

    Tutima Balances Titanium with Gold, and Hand-Wound Tradition

    Tutima’s new Patria pairs a grade-five titanium case with 18K gold details and its hand-wound Calibre 617. A 60-hour reserve and Glashütte finishing keep the material contrast rooted in the movement.

    WatchTime

  3. 03

    FounderFest Makes Independent Watchmaking a Live Stage

    Time+Tide’s FounderFest brings independent makers, collectors and live music together across New York, London and Melbourne. The associated mechanical watch collaborations turn the release calendar into a more communal, in-person format.

    Monochrome

September 19, 2026
  1. 01

    Studio Underd0g brings a monopusher chronograph to Dubai.

    Studio Underd0g and Ahmed Seddiqi introduce the 100-piece Chan3ad with Eastern Arabic numerals and a two-layer fish-inspired dial. At its core is the manually wound Sellita SW510 M monopusher chronograph movement with 63 hours of power reserve.

    Hodinkee

  2. 02

    Parmigiani lets the calibre carry the colour.

    Parmigiani Fleurier presents the 30-piece Tonda PF Skeleton in rose gold and Milano Blue. The openworked automatic PF777 movement, with twin barrels, finishing and structural depth, becomes the watch’s true visual centre.

    WatchTime

  3. 03

    Czapek turns a novel into a mechanical dial.

    Czapek’s Promenade L’Écume des Jours translates Boris Vian’s pianocktail and water-lily imagery into Grand Feu enamel, hand-painted details and the automatic SXH5.1 micro-rotor movement. The edition is limited to 18 pieces.

    Monochrome Watches

September 18, 2026
  1. 01

    Watches and Wonders expands its independent horizon.

    Watches and Wonders 2027 will expand to 75 exhibiting brands, with Berneron, Singer Reimagined and Porsche Design among the first-time participants. The growth gives independent mechanical watchmaking a more formal and concentrated international stage.

    Hodinkee

  2. 02

    Glashütte Original finds a mechanical rhythm in the seventies.

    Glashütte Original’s new The 70s line draws on vinyl, hi-fi and 1970s design, while remaining anchored by the manufacture Calibre 36 automatic movement, its 100-hour power reserve and traditional Saxon finishing.

    WatchTime

  3. 03

    Neode begins with architecture, not decoration.

    New independent brand Neode launches its N01 First Edition with a Voutilainen-made case, a deeply reworked manual Chronode movement and an eight-day power reserve. Limited to 25 pieces, it puts structure, proportion and movement architecture ahead of ornament.

    Monochrome Watches

September 17, 2026
  1. 01

    Singer Reimagined makes GMT a matter of architecture.

    Singer’s 1970 DualTrack places dual-time reading inside a slimmer 1970s-inspired integrated-bracelet case. Its manual-winding ST5001 movement uses four barrels to deliver six days of power reserve.

    Fratello

  2. 02

    Louis Moinet turns lunar time into a mechanical landscape.

    Moon Meteor combines miniature painting, meteorite material and a high-precision moon-phase display in a 12-piece titanium edition. Its automatic LM113 movement drives a lunar cycle claimed accurate to one day in 122 years.

    Monochrome Watches

  3. 03

    Straum gives its integrated-bracelet formula an outdoor life.

    Norwegian independent Straum takes its integrated-bracelet design into more capable territory with titanium, 100m water resistance and stronger lume. The Expedition retains the automatic La Joux-Perret G100 movement with a 68-hour power reserve.

    WatchTime

September 16, 2026
  1. 01

    Rolex reopens the calendar conversation.

    Rolex revisits the historic Padellone with a new Perpetual model featuring an instantaneous annual calendar and moonphase. It brings a more complicated proposition back into the brand’s dress-watch narrative.

    Hodinkee

  2. 02

    TAG Heuer makes the Carrera Sport more wearable.

    The new Carrera Chronograph Sport shrinks from 44mm to 42mm while retaining the TH20-00 automatic column-wheel chronograph and its 80-hour power reserve. It is a mechanical update shaped as much by daily proportions as by racing credentials.

    Monochrome Watches

  3. 03

    Complications take the stage in New York.

    WatchTime New York will bring minute repeaters, perpetual calendars and tourbillons to the foreground this October. The programme shows how technical watchmaking and independent brands are becoming central to the fair’s proposition.

    WatchTime

September 15, 2026
  1. 01

    Serena Williams Reframes the Royal Oak Chronograph

    Audemars Piguet’s 38mm Royal Oak Selfwinding Chronograph for Serena Williams pairs white ceramic with a pink mother-of-pearl dial. Its integrated, column-wheel Calibre 6401 keeps the 500-piece edition firmly rooted in mechanical watchmaking.

    Hodinkee

  2. 02

    Six Omega Stopwatches Preserve a Lost Racing Circuit

    A fitted set of six Omega professional mechanical stopwatches, engraved LEVIN M.R.C., preserves a direct link to a vanished New Zealand racing circuit. The objects show how mechanical timing equipment once formed part of motorsport’s working infrastructure.

    Fratello

  3. 03

    Rolex Turns Four Daytonas into a Seasonal Charity Series

    Rolex created four unique Cosmograph Daytonas around the seasons, combining precious metals, natural-stone dials and gem setting. Powered by the automatic Calibre 4131, the watches will be auctioned by Phillips to benefit two charitable organisations.

    WatchTime

September 14, 2026
  1. 01

    De Bethune rejects scarcity as a shortcut to desire.

    New CEO Antoine Pin says the brand will not treat limited production or secondary-market appreciation as its central proposition, instead building demand through art, innovation and long-term product value. It is a clear challenge to the idea that rarity alone creates desirability.

    WatchPro

  2. 02

    Cartier brings the Roadster back without making it a museum piece.

    Cartier’s Roadster revival updates the early sports-watch design with the maison’s current quick-change strap system. The point is not simply a reissue, but to bring an overlooked line back into the brand’s present-day product language.

    Haute Time

  3. 03

    Citizen turns fifty years of Eco-Drive into two atmospheric ATTESA watches.

    Citizen marks Eco-Drive’s fiftieth anniversary with Green Flash and Purple Aurora ATTESA limited editions, each capped at 1,500 pieces. Structural colour film and vapour deposition create the light effects, while the F950 movement provides rapid satellite-signal reception.

    Citizen

September 13, 2026
  1. 01

    Rolex still leads, but secondary-market attention is spreading.

    Chrono24’s new report shows Rolex remains the largest brand on its platform, but has lost share in every major price segment since 2023 as Cartier, Patek Philippe, Audemars Piguet and Vacheron Constantin gain ground. The pandemic-era Rolex premium has also largely unwound.

    JCK

  2. 02

    Tudor’s Northflag returns with a sharper identity.

    The new Northflag continues Tudor’s tool-watch line while adding GMT functionality and a more complete contemporary specification. It shows the brand using historical threads to strengthen its range beyond anniversary-led storytelling.

    Hodinkee

  3. 03

    Ebel’s 115th anniversary makes room for both mechanics and jewellery.

    Ebel marks 115 years with two limited Sport Classic watches: a Dubois Dépraz automatic chronograph and a gemstone-set quartz model. Together they make a direct case for the brand’s old idea that function and beauty can serve distinct collectors in parallel.

    WatchTime

September 12, 2026
  1. 01

    Swiss watchmakers face a price-and-value reality check.

    Deloitte’s survey of 6,500 consumers across 13 markets suggests buyers put price-to-value ahead of the industry’s constant flow of novelties. It also highlights a growing mismatch between brands’ focus on mono-brand boutiques and customers’ preference for choice and comparison.

    WatchPro

  2. 02

    Bangkok Watch Week signals Southeast Asia’s growing horological pull.

    The second Siam Paragon Bangkok Watch Week will run September 22–27 with more than 45 participating brands—more than double the first edition. Its growth shows how watch fairs are increasingly reaching toward collector communities beyond the traditional European circuit.

    Hodinkee

  3. 03

    A. Lange & Söhne pairs new complications with the world of classic cars.

    At Hampton Court Palace’s Concours of Elegance, Lange presented the new platinum Triple Split and two 1815 Up/Down special editions. For a small high-watchmaking house, the event shows how cultural settings can frame craft and heritage more effectively than a standalone launch.

    A. Lange & Söhne

September 11, 2026
  1. 01

    WatchPro USA names the people and businesses shaping the American watch market.

    The 2026 WatchPro USA Awards recognised achievement across retail, brand building, customer experience and industry leadership—not merely new releases. Its winners offer a useful snapshot of the businesses and people setting the pace in a growing American watch market.

    WatchPro USA

  2. 02

    Jaeger-LeCoultre brings fifteen complications into one Grande Sonnerie.

    Jaeger-LeCoultre’s one-off Duomètre Grande Complication à Grande Sonnerie unites a Westminster-style grande sonnerie, minute repeater, perpetual calendar and flying tourbillon in Calibre 182. It is a forceful reminder that extreme mechanical watchmaking remains central to the maison’s identity.

    Jaeger-LeCoultre

  3. 03

    Panerai marks a century in Florence with an eight-day Luminor.

    For the centenary of its historic Florence boutique, Panerai revisits a 1960s reference in the limited Luminor Firenze 8 Giorni PAM01794. The release uses the brand’s familiar eight-day hand-wound movement to connect its modern identity with its Florentine origins.

    Fratello

September 10, 2026
  1. 01

    Gérald Genta returns to the minute repeater with restraint.

    The revived maison’s Geneva Minute Repeater Golden Obsidian pairs a 40mm yellow-gold cushion case with a natural golden-obsidian dial, at roughly 9.6mm thick. It continues the LVMH-backed revival through thin dress watches and serious complications rather than a rapid expansion of references.

    Hodinkee

  2. 02

    Legare revives an overlooked double-balance idea.

    The new independent brand’s Chapter I AGP Inverted draws on Albert-Gustave Piguet’s 1932 double-balance chronometer project. Its inverted LC1-AGP-01 movement makes the mechanism the visual centre, showing a different route to distinction than another conventional tourbillon.

    HOROMAG

  3. 03

    Timex brings the premium GMT down to 38mm.

    Timex’s Atelier GMT Première M1a reduces the collection to a 38mm case while retaining a Swiss movement and double-domed sapphire crystal. It is a useful sign that the smaller-case trend is reaching a genuinely broad consumer brand, not only high-end houses.

    Notebookcheck

September 9, 2026
  1. 01

    King Seiko lets Tokyo enter the dial.

    King Seiko’s new steel Vanac HKF005 uses a dark, speckled dial to evoke Tokyo’s night skyline. It is a regular-production release—not another scarcity exercise—and shows how a familiar sports-watch format can gain character through surface rather than complication.

    Fratello

  2. 02

    Breitling turns a Miles Davis tribute into a collecting proposition.

    The 500-piece Navitimer B09 Miles Davis draws on the musician’s ref. 806, with a hand-wound chronograph and a reworked Air Racer bracelet. More tellingly, the 50-set top tier includes entry to Breitling’s forthcoming Social Club—an increasingly experiential approach to ownership.

    Hodinkee

  3. 03

    Awake makes lacquer the point, not the decoration.

    Awake’s new Son Mai Frosted Leaf colours continue its use of Vietnamese lacquer, natural pigments and silver leaf, with roughly 15 hours of work in each dial. At around 100 pieces per colour each year, it is a useful example of an independent brand building distinction through craft at a relatively accessible level.

    Monochrome Watches

September 8, 2026
  1. 01

    Nomos turns its dials into an atelier

    Nomos’s made-to-order Twice Unique collection treats five familiar models as canvases for nine artist-led dial designs. Each commission pairs a steel watch with an 18K gold counterpart, favouring handwork and individuality over conventional limited-edition logic.

    Fratello

  2. 02

    The GPHG finalists set the stage for November

    The 2026 GPHG finalists are now public ahead of the November 7 ceremony. Revised, non-gendered categories and a new jury president signal an awards institution seeking a broader global audience—while giving smaller brands a meaningful stage.

    Hodinkee

  3. 03

    Rolex sends four unique Daytonas to charity auction

    Four one-off, season-inspired gem-set Daytonas will be sold separately by Phillips in London on September 28. The proceeds support the Federer Foundation and Re:wild, placing a rare public Rolex project at the intersection of craftsmanship and philanthropy.

    Fratello

September 7, 2026
  1. 01

    Geneva Watch Days signals a renewed search for volume

    With 71 brands and nearly 400 new watches, the fair pointed to smaller cases, bolder design and a renewed interest in more attainable price points—an industry response to shrinking unit sales and younger buyers.

    Luxus PlusForbes

  2. 02

    Hermès’s watchmaking ambitions meet a perception problem

    After two decades of investment, Hermès is building real watchmaking scale. Yet its association with Birkin access and weaker resale values shows that technical capability alone does not create collector confidence.

    The Wall Street Journal

  3. 03

    Watch fairs multiply as the industry looks for new audiences

    Geneva Watch Days continues to grow, new collector-led fairs are emerging in the US, and MCH Group is preparing Basilia for 2027. Physical shows are becoming places for brands, media and communities to meet—not merely order-taking events.

    Financial Times
